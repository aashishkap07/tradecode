## 2. The questions and my answers

### Q1. Is this taxed as a "virtual digital asset" (30% flat, losses ignored)?

**My opinion: no. It is business income, taxed at your slab rate. But the risk is real (section 3).**

The VDA rules (s.115BBH of the 1961 Act; carried into the Income-tax Act, 2025, which took effect on 1 April 2026) tax
**income from the transfer of a VDA**. A VDA is a cryptographically generated token, code or number with value. From
1 April 2026 it also includes "crypto-assets" recorded on a cryptographically secured distributed ledger. Applied to
your trade:

1. **What you hold is a contract, not a token.** A futures position on Delta or CoinDCX is an entry in the exchange's
   own database, a promise to settle a price difference in rupees. It is not generated by cryptographic means and does
   not live on a distributed ledger.
2. **Nothing crypto is ever transferred to you or by you.** You deposit rupees, and you get rupees back. "Transfer of a
   VDA", the event the 30% section taxes, never happens.
3. **The exchanges treat it this way.**
   - Delta Exchange India states its crypto futures and options "do not qualify as trading Virtual Digital Assets, so
     the 30% VDA tax is not applicable".
   - CoinDCX's help page says its INR-margined futures have no TDS and no VDA involvement, and are taxed at the slab
     rate.
   - Neither deducts the 1% TDS that applies to VDA transfers.
4. **The government has not notified derivatives as VDAs**, although the law lets it do so.
5. **Against this view:**
   - some advisers argue a contract whose value comes from crypto should be treated like crypto;
   - Mudrex's own FAQ takes the 30% view;
   - **there is no CBDT circular and no court ruling either way.**

**Analogy:** betting on the price of gold with a friend and settling in cash is not buying gold. The tax on selling gold
doesn't apply, because no gold ever changed hands. But until the tax office says so in writing, someone there could
argue otherwise.

### Q2. What kind of business income: speculative or not?

**Speculative business income** (Schedule BP of ITR-3).
- A "speculative transaction" is a contract for a commodity or shares settled otherwise than by actual delivery
  (s.43(5) of the 1961 Act; s.66(31) of the 2025 Act).
- The exception for derivatives covers only those on recognised stock exchanges regulated by SEBI. Crypto exchanges are
  not.
- So the safe classification is speculative. This is also how the plan has been modelled since C528.

**Why that's fine for this trade:**
- Both legs, on both exchanges, are speculative transactions of the **same** business. So a loss on one exchange is set
  off against the gain on the other within the same year.
- The tax falls only on the **net** profit. This is the "T1" reading the research uses: +3.71% a month after tax at
  costs ×5 on today's coin list.

**Losses:**
- A speculative loss can be set off only against speculative profit (the 2025 Act's s.113, formerly s.73).
- It carries forward **4 years**, but **only if you file your return on time**.

### Q3. Fees, GST and running costs

These are business expenses, deducted from the profit:
- exchange fees;
- the 18% GST on them (you are not GST-registered, so you can't reclaim it);
- the rupee transfer charges;
- a fair share of any running costs (the server, if you pay for it).

You do **not** need to register for GST: trading on your own account is not a supply.

### Q4. TDS

- **None is expected** on INR futures at Delta or CoinDCX.
- Before filing, check your AIS / Form 26AS. If any TDS was deducted, it is credited against your tax.

### Q5. Your tax regime

### Q6. Advance tax (paying during the year)

Advance tax is due when the year's tax not covered by TDS exceeds **Rs 10,000**.
- **Tax year 2026-27** (to 31 March 2027): only the February pilot and one live month. The expected tax is about
  Rs 1,000-2,000, so no advance tax. Pay it as self-assessment tax before filing.
- **Tax year 2027-28:**
  - at $1,000, if the trade earns what the research expects, the profit is roughly Rs 50,000-70,000 before tax, and
    the tax roughly **Rs 15,000-22,000**;
  - so advance tax is due: **15 Jun 2027 (15%), 15 Sep (45%), 15 Dec (75%), 15 Mar 2028 (100%)**;
  - paying late costs about 1% a month in interest on the shortfall.
- **Simple habit:** each month, move **31.2% of that month's net profit** to a separate savings account, and pay from
  it on those dates. I'll calculate the amounts in the monthly review.

## 3. The risk, in numbers

The research, re-run today on the plan's 71 coins (two years, 10 pairs of 10%):

| how the tax department reads it | after tax per month (costs ×5) | normal costs |
|---|---|---|
| **business income, both legs netted (my opinion)** | **+3.71%** | **+4.33%** |
| the CoinDCX leg alone taxed as a VDA | +0.23% | +0.91% |
| **both legs taxed as VDAs** (each winning leg taxed 30%, every losing leg ignored) | **−2.95%** | **−2.21%** |

**What this means:**
- The trade works only under the business-income reading. Under the worst reading, the tax on each pair's winning leg
  is larger than the whole profit, because each pair always has one winning and one losing leg.
- **In rupees:** if the department ever applied the worst reading to a full year at $1,000, the extra tax could be
  around **Rs 60,000-80,000 for that year**, plus interest. With proper disclosure, penalty is unlikely.
- **How likely is it?** I judge the business-income reading **more likely than not to be accepted**. The contract is not
  a token, no VDA is transferred, the exchanges don't deduct TDS, and nothing has been notified. But it is not settled,
  and I won't pretend otherwise.

**What protects you:**
1. **Disclosure.** Report it clearly as speculative business income, with a short note: INR-settled derivative
   contracts, no transfer of a VDA. A bona fide, disclosed position is the standard defence against penalty.
2. **Size.** At about Rs 1 lakh, the worst case is a bill you could pay, not a ruin. Don't scale up beyond $1,000 until
   the law is clarified or a full tax year has passed without a query.
3. **A tax watch.**
   - Every monthly review checks for any CBDT circular or notification on crypto derivatives.
   - **The Union Budget on 1 February 2027** is the one to watch: a Finance Bill could change the rules. The go/no-go on
     20 February reads it first.
   - If derivatives are brought under the VDA rules, the plan stops before real money.
