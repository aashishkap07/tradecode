1

Your money sits in two jars

Your $1,000 is split between two Indian exchanges. Think of each as a separate jar. Money can't jump from one jar to the other by itself.

2

A bet on a price: “up” or “down”

The coin ABC costs $10. You can bet $100 on it, the value of 10 coins, without owning any.

  • A “bet up” (traders say long) wins when the price rises.
  • A “bet down” (traders say short) wins when the price falls.

The exchange keeps some of your jar as a deposit (margin) while a bet is open, like a security deposit on a flat. In our plan each $500 jar holds about $1,000 of bets. That's called 2× leverage.

3

Funding is rent

These bets never expire, so the exchange needs a way to keep their price honest. Every 8 hours, the crowded side pays the quiet side a small amount. That payment is called funding. Think of it as rent.

The key fact: Delta India's crowd mostly bets coins up, so on Delta the rent for being a down-bettor is high. Pi42 follows Binance's worldwide crowd, where the same coin's rent is usually much lower. The difference between the two rents is the rent gap, and it's what the bot earns.
4

One coin, two bets: “legs” and a “pair”

The bot makes two opposite bets on the same coin, of the same size, one on each exchange. Each bet is called a leg, and the two legs together make a pair, like the two legs of a pair of trousers.

Ten pairs: the whole plan

The bot does this with up to 10 different coins at once, about $100 a leg. It picks coins whose rent gap has been big over the last week, and swaps a pair out when its gap shrinks.

The two-jar problem, and the fix

All 10 pairs lean the same way: bet down on Delta, bet up on Pi42. Imagine all 10 coins jump 20% in one day:

  • Each Delta bet loses $20, so the Delta jar drops by $200.
  • Each Pi42 bet gains $20, so the Pi42 jar grows by $200.
  • The total is still $1,000, but the money is in the wrong jar.
In paper the bot moves the money itself. When it's live, you'll do it as a bank transfer: withdraw rupees from one exchange and deposit them on the other. The bot will tell you how much and which way. This happened for real last night: the Delta jar was $328 and the Pi42 jar $672, so this morning's run moves about $170 across.
8

Why we left Binance

Same imaginary pair, one month. A coin jumps: the Delta bet loses $50, the other bet gains $50, and the rent gap adds $6. Your real profit is $6. Now see what tax does, depending on where the second leg sits:

  • The law on sending money abroad: under RBI's remittance rules, an Indian resident can't send money abroad to use as a deposit for these bets on a foreign exchange. Delta India and Pi42 take rupees from your Indian bank, so nothing leaves India.
  • Simpler paperwork: no 1% TDS on rupee bets. The profit goes on your ITR-3 as business income.
  • What we gave up: Pi42 lists fewer coins, so the expected return before tax fell from about 72% to about 50% a year. You still keep much more after tax, and it's legal.
  • Binance still helps, without your money: Pi42 copies Binance's prices, so the bot reads prices and rent from Binance. Binance also runs the paper “book” experiment.

This is the safe tax reading from your stand-in CA. I'm not a registered CA, and the rules on crypto futures aren't fully settled.
