## What it is, in plain words

On paper, the bot is its own bank. It decides what it bought, what it paid in
fees and what funding it owed, and nobody checks. With real money, Bitget is
the bank, and the bot must agree with Bitget to the cent. Pending task #2
listed five places where paper and a real account can disagree. All five are
now handled.

| # | the risk with real money | what C492 does |
|---|---|---|
| a | **Funding counted twice.** Paper books an estimate of funding at each settlement, while Bitget charges or pays the real amount. Live, both would land. | Live, the paper estimate is **switched off**. The bot reads Bitget's own account bills (type `contract_settle_fee`) and books each one exactly once, by its bill ID, to the position that paid or earned it. It survives restarts. |
| b | **The ledger drifting from the account.** Slippage, fee tiers and anything Bitget does that the bot did not see. | Every 60 s (and before and after each rebalance) the bot reads Bitget's balance and positions. **Bitget is the truth.** The ledger takes Bitget's wallet figure every time. A gap above $0.50 (or 0.1% of equity) is logged loudly. If a position differs, it is re-read 2 s later and the book takes Bitget's quantity, with a loud "correction" line. |
| c | **Wrong account settings.** In hedge mode Bitget rejects the bot's orders. The wrong margin mode changes the risk. | Before trading at all, the account must be in **one-way** mode: it is switched if the account is flat, otherwise the bot waits and says why. Before the first order on each coin, it sets and reads back **cross margin at 5x**. A coin Bitget will not switch is skipped, loudly. |
| d | **Partial fills.** On paper every market order fills in full; live, it may not. | The book records what actually filled, never what was asked for. A flip from long to short opens the short **only after the long is fully closed**. A flatten retries twice and then shouts "CHECK BITGET BY HAND". |
| e | **Contract rules changing.** Step size, minimum order, maintenance. | Before each rebalance the bot reads Bitget's live contract table. It uses Bitget's own step, minimum quantity, minimum dollar order and biggest market order (larger orders are split). It opens nothing on a contract that is not `normal`; a `limit_open` contract can only be reduced. This also runs in paper, so paper refuses what live would refuse. |

### Why cross margin (a trader's decision, made explicit)

The research held every position through its ups and downs, with no stop per
coin. With **isolated** margin at 5x, Bitget would liquidate a coin after about
a 19% move against it. An altcoin held for two weeks moves that much
routinely. That would add a stop the research never had, plus Bitget's
liquidation fee.

With **cross** margin, the whole account backs the book, which is how a
diversified book is normally run. The book's stop is the **month guard**: it
flattens everything if the month loses more than the dial allows. It is
checked every 20 seconds on live equity.

The switch is `C488_MARGIN_MODE = 'cross'`. One trap was found and blocked:
ccxt turns Bitget's own word `'crossed'` into an **isolated** order. The code
accepts either spelling and always sends the right one, and a test pins it.

## How it was verified

- **`omega_c492_test.py`, 69 checks, all pass.** The bot's real order code runs
  against a simulated Bitget account. Each order is turned into the exact
  Bitget request by the installed ccxt (4.5.84, on real market data saved in
  `test_fixtures/c492_bitget_btc_eth.json`), and the simulated account acts on
  that request. It covers:
  - one-way netting and margin-mode checks;
  - reduce-only orders;
  - fees, funding bills (including 150 bills across two pages) and partial
    fills;
  - lost read-backs, vanished positions and a passing one-read blip;
  - a whole 36-coin live rebalance. It ended with Bitget holding exactly the
    book, coin by coin, and the ledger equal to Bitget's wallet to the cent.
- **Against the real Bitget API** (no key of yours was used):
  - All 7 private endpoints the code calls exist. Bitget answered "API key does
    not exist" (40037); a made-up path got 404 as a control.
  - The bot's own preflight with a wrong key gave LIVE NOT READY, named the
    key, and traded nothing.
  - The live contract table: 805 contracts, all `normal`. Bitget's steps and
    minimums equal ccxt's on all 805, and on all 40 of today's candidates.
    ETH is the lumpiest step at $26.94.
- **Each key test was proven able to fail.** It was run on a copy with the
  fault put back: the old flip (the ledger thought it was flat while Bitget held
  a short), and the paper funding estimate booked live (the double count).
  Both were caught.
- **A bug in my own first draft was caught on review:** every normal reduction
  would have logged a false "partial" warning. It is fixed and pinned by a
  test.
- **Regression:** every older test file passes and the sweeps show nothing new.
  A full paper boot on live Bitget data was clean: the first book was 9
  positions, 0.44× gross, close to the server's real first book of 9 positions
  at 0.41×.

## When you want real money (not now: after #1 passes and #3 is done)

Going live takes two separate deliberate steps, so neither can happen by
accident:
1. **Me:** a one-line commit that sets `C488_LIVE_OK = True`, which you ask for.
2. **You:** `OMEGA_MODE=live` in the service.

The order:
1. **Pending #1 passes** (the paper check; reminder set for 29 Sep 06:45 IST).
2. **Pending #3, security:**
   - rotate the control token;
   - rotate the NewsData key;
   - create a new Bitget API key: **trade only, withdrawals disabled, IP
     allow-listed to this server**;
   - the account must be the **classic** USDT-M futures account, in single-asset
     mode. A Unified Trading Account is refused by the preflight.
3. **Update ccxt on the server** and check the margin-mode mapping on *its*
   version:

   ```bash
   sudo -u omega /home/omega/omega/venv/bin/pip install -U ccxt
   cd /home/omega/omega && sudo -u omega venv/bin/python -c "import ccxt,json;F=json.load(open('test_fixtures/c492_bitget_btc_eth.json'));e=ccxt.bitget({'options':{'defaultType':'swap'}});e.set_markets(list(F['markets'].values()));r=e.create_order_request('BTC/USDT:USDT','market','buy',0.001,None,{'marginMode':'cross'});print(ccxt.__version__,r['marginMode'],r.get('tradeSide'))"
   ```

   It must print the version, then `crossed None`.
4. **Put the key in the data folder** (paste your three values in the editor;
   they never go in a chat or in git):

   ```bash
   sudo -u omega nano /home/omega/omega/data/api_keys.json
   ```

   The contents: `{"api_key": "...", "api_secret": "...", "api_password": "..."}`

   ```bash
   sudo chmod 600 /home/omega/omega/data/api_keys.json
   sudo chown omega:omega /home/omega/omega/data/api_keys.json
   ```
5. **Move money only before the start.** While the book runs live, a withdrawal
   looks like a loss, and above 15% of the month's starting equity it trips the
   month guard. A deposit looks like a gain.
6. **Start small:** dial 10% at $250. The paper book held 9 positions averaging
   about $13 ($118.63 / 9) at dial 15%. At dial 10% that is about $9 each,
   still above the $6 floor. At dial 5% it is about $4.40, below Bitget's $5
   minimum, so most positions would vanish and the live book would stop
   resembling the tested one.
7. **Switch the service with an override.** Never copy `deploy/omega.service`
   over the installed unit.

   ```bash
   cp -a /home/omega/omega/data /home/omega/omega/data.paper-backup
   sudo systemctl edit omega
   ```

   In the editor add:

   ```
   [Service]
   Environment=OMEGA_MODE=live
   Environment=OMEGA_CAPITAL=<your USDT-M futures balance>
   Environment=OMEGA_MAX_DD=10
   ```

   Then start fresh (live begins flat and does not inherit the paper book):

   ```bash
   sudo -u omega touch /home/omega/omega/data/FRESH_START
   sudo systemctl restart omega
   sleep 90
   sudo journalctl -u omega -n 200 --no-pager | grep -E "OMEGA C4|LIVE|C488|Traceback"
   ```

   **What you should see:**
   - `C488 LIVE READY: one-way position mode; cross margin at 5x …`;
   - at the first rebalance, one `ready -- one-way, crossed margin, 5x` line per
     coin, then the fills;
   - the dashboard's live line, with drift near $0.00.

   **What must stop you:** `LIVE NOT READY`, `SYNC FAILING`, or any `the book now
   follows Bitget` / `the ledger now follows Bitget` line in the first days.
   Each one means paper and live disagree, and it is worth a look before the
   next rebalance.
