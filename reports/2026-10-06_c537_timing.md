# How often should the rent-gap trade decide? (Round 19, C537)

6 Oct 2026. You sent a note on how rent (funding) works and asked whether the bot uses those
principles in the most profitable way. Your example was how often it should look at the rent gap and
decide to close: "fixed daily reviews … or … the frequency … is the most profitable on average? check
other things too in detail".

The rules and the pass bar were written down and pushed **before** any number was computed
(`research/c537_preregistration.md`, commit 7ba0b52). The test is `research/c537_timing.py` and its
output is `research/c537_timing.txt` / `.json`.

## 1. The answer in one line

**Once a day at 06:00 IST stays.** None of the 11 changes beat it by enough to be sure it is not
luck. Checking every 8 hours came closest: about **$34 a year more** on $1,000, but not proven. It
gets re-tested on fresh data on 2 December.

## 2. How the test worked

Two years of real rent records, **2024-10 to 2026-09**, on the coins the bot trades. That is 54 coins:
Pi42 lists them, Delta trades over $100k a day, and the price moves together on both.

**What was simulated:**
- Every **hour**, every rent payment as it actually happened on each exchange.
- **18% GST** on any rent paid.
- **Trading fees** on every open and close, at the quoted fees, and again at **5× fees** in case the
  real fees turn out higher.
- Whole Delta contracts, on $1,000.
- One change at a time, against today's rule.

**The bar a change had to clear (fixed in advance):**
1. at least **+$30 a year** more than today's rule;
2. a strength-of-evidence score (t) of **2.5 or more**;
3. better in at least 3 of the 4 half-years, including the latest;
4. still no worse than today's rule at **5× fees**.

The figures are rent earned minus fees, before tax. The two bets on each coin cancel each other's
price moves, so prices are left out. The earlier full study, with prices, put the trade at about
+50%/yr at 5× fees. This test gives today's rule +52.7%/yr at 5× fees, so the two agree.

## 4. Why checking more often does not pay

**Analogy:** a gardener can check the plants every morning or every hour. On a hot afternoon some
leaves droop for an hour, then recover by evening. A gardener who pulls up every drooping plant
loses good plants and pays for new seedlings.

**In numbers:**
- About **1 in 5 rent payments** on a pair the bot holds goes the wrong way. That costs about $95 a
  year on $1,000, and it is already counted in the table.
- Deciding every hour cuts that to about $74. But the bot also closes pairs whose gap would have come
  back, then pays fees to reopen them. The net gain is +$5 a year.

**The gap between the two exchanges is sticky.** A week after the bot measures a coin's gap, about
**94% of it is still there**, on the same side 93–95% of the time:

| gap now (a year) | coin-days | left a week later | same side |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10–20% | 5,993 | 94% | 93% |
| 20–40% | 4,801 | 94% | 94% |
| 40–80% | 4,207 | 96% | 95% |
| 80%+ | 3,572 | 88% | 94% |

**Analogy:** the gap moves like the tide, not like waves. Once a day is enough to follow a tide. A
pair is held for **18 days** on average (the median).

## 5. Your note's principles, and what the bot does with each

| the principle | what the bot does | checked |
|---|---|---|
| **Rent ties the bet price to the real price; the crowd that pushes it away pays.** | The bot doesn't bet on the price. On each coin it holds one bet up on one exchange and one bet down on the other, so price moves cancel. It earns because each exchange has its **own crowd**. | Rent − fees +$671 a year on $1,000 (two years). |
| **Ola/Uber surge.** | Two taxi stands: stand A has a 2× surge, stand B doesn't. You **drive** at A, so you earn the surge, and **ride** at B, so you pay the normal fare. You keep the difference, whichever way the city's traffic goes. | The bot opens only when the surge gap is **≥ 20% a year**. |
| **Paying rent pushes the crowded side out; receiving rent invites careful traders who hedge.** | **The bot is that careful trader.** Its hedge is the opposite bet on the other exchange, not the real coin. It needs no coin wallet, and the money sits in the two futures accounts. | Holding the real coin and collecting rent pays only the "base rent" (below), about +2% a year on the typical coin, less than fees and tax. That experiment ran as "cash-and-carry" and is folded away. |
| **The bigger the gap, the stronger the pull (rubber band).** | True **within** one exchange: rent pulls the bet price back to the real price. The gap **between** two exchanges is different: their crowds stay different for weeks. | 94% of a gap is still there a week later. Only the biggest gaps (80%+ a year) shrink noticeably, to 88%. |
| **Nobody in the middle keeps it; the exchange is the referee.** | True for the rent itself, but two others take a cut: the referee's **trading fee** and the government's **18% GST on rent paid**. The bot books both. | Fees $36 a year; GST is in the rent figures. |
| **A tiny default rent: up-bettors pay a little on calm days (interest).** | **Both exchanges charge the same default:** 0.01% every 8 hours (about 11% a year), or 0.005% on 4-hour coins. On calm days the two cancel, the gap is 0, and the bot does nothing. So the default rent neither helps nor hurts. | Binance/Pi42's rate was the default on **45%** of payments. Delta's commonest rates are the same 0.01% (20%) and 0.005% (10%). Typical coin over a year: Delta −3.1%, Pi42 +2.3%; gap −3.8%, far below the 20% bar. |
| **The side the crowd is on pays the side the crowd isn't on.** | The bot's rule exactly. Where Delta's rent is higher, it bets **down on Delta** (collects) and **up on Pi42** (pays less). Where Pi42's is higher, the other way round. | — |

## 6. Other things checked

**The two clocks: watching vs. deciding.** The bot already runs at two speeds:

| clock | how often | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| **watching** | hourly now | rent "waiting to be added", each account's health (the 65% and 50% lines) |
| **deciding** | once a day | open or close pairs, move money between the accounts |

- The test shows deciding once a day is right.
- For **live money, the health check moves to every 5 minutes**, with a phone alert. That is already
  gate G5 (an alert within 5 minutes); it is now also written into build step B3 in the roadmap.
- Each side holds about 1× its money in bets. For a side to lose half in the hour between checks,
  its coins would have to move about 50% against it in that hour. Hourly is safe for paper; live
  gets 5 minutes anyway.

**When rent is paid.**
- Both exchanges pay rent at 00, 04, 08, 12, 16 and 20 UTC: 05:30, 09:30, 13:30, 17:30, 21:30 and
  01:30 IST.
- 06:00 IST is **30 minutes after** the day's biggest payment (05:30 IST). A pair being closed has
  just collected its last rent. A new pair is never charged for a payment it didn't hold through.
- Nothing is missed or counted twice.

**When to close.**
- Closing only when the gap changes side costs $73 a year.
- Closing at 15% adds $21 at quoted fees, but loses at 5× fees.
- **10% stays.**

**When to open.** 15% or 30%: no real difference (−$1 / +$17, the size of noise). **20% stays.**

**The averaging window.**
- **3 days** gains $74 a year at quoted fees, but trades 68% more (284 opens vs 169) and **loses**
  $28 at 5× fees. It is fragile while Pi42's real fees are unmeasured (the February pilot measures
  them).
- **14 days** is too slow: −$147.
- **7 stays.**

**Number of pairs.** 15 smaller pairs instead of 10 costs $110 a year. The extra 5 are weaker gaps.
**10 stays.**

## 7. What changes

- **The trading rule: nothing.** The bot stays at C536.
- **Roadmap:**
  - step B3 now says the account-health check runs every 5 minutes when live;
  - the 2 December research refresh now re-tests "every 8 h" and "3-day average". It uses Oct–Nov
    as fresh data the test has never seen, at the same bar. If either passes then, the bot changes
    before the March start.
