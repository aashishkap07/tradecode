## 1. The intraday shadow's weakness (Part A)

The shadow gives every coin in its cohort the same weight, so one coin moving
10–30% in an hour dominates it. That is what happened with 龙虾 on 2 Oct.

The test ran on Binance's hourly data, 2021-07 → 2026-08, with the research
engine unchanged and the same walk-forward model:
- **M1:** equal weight, as now;
- **M1v:** inverse-volatility weights (each coin in its own volatility units,
  the book's principle);
- **M1x:** coins swinging more than 3× the median left out.

| | M1 (now) | **M1v (inverse vol)** | M1x (filter) |
|---|---|---|---|
| typical hourly swing | 0.283% | **0.199% (−30%)** | 0.212% |
| worst hour | −7.26% | **−5.48%** | −4.73% |
| worst day | −7.69% | **−6.04%** | −7.74% |
| fat tails (kurtosis) | 2,134 | **370** | 29 |
| the signal BEFORE costs (Sharpe) | 1.31 | **1.74** | 1.64 |
| **net of costs** | **−218%/yr** | −228%/yr | −217%/yr |

- **The fix works on risk:**
  - In the 龙虾 hour inverse volatility would have held about 0.6% instead of
    4.7%, losing about −0.08% instead of −0.59%. 龙虾 was swinging 7.3× the
    median coin.
  - Over 5 years the swings are 30% smaller and the raw signal is better.
- **It cannot rescue the model:** it pays about **250%/yr in trading costs**
  for a 30%/yr raw edge. No weighting changes that. This confirms the
  original research (round 4).
- **It failed the pre-registered bar** on one condition: "Sharpe difference
  ≥ 0". **That condition was my mistake.** When a strategy loses money,
  lowering its volatility makes its Sharpe *more* negative, so the rule
  punishes an improvement. The bar was fixed in advance, so M1v is **not
  added**, and future bars compare such strategies on gross Sharpe or at
  equal risk.
- **Recommendation:** leave the shadow running as the record of a failed
  idea, or switch it off (`C489_SHADOW=False`). It never touches money.

## 3. The new finding: Delta vs Binance funding (Part B)

**What it is.** Perpetual futures charge "funding" between longs and shorts.
On Delta Exchange India the same coin often costs longs far more (or less)
than on Binance. One week's real numbers (raw APIs, 2 Oct):

| coin | Delta, longs pay | Binance, longs pay | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| ORDER | 158%/yr | 12%/yr | 146%/yr |
| AIN | 228%/yr | 99%/yr | 129%/yr |
| KAITO | 133%/yr | 11%/yr | 122%/yr |

Delta's price for these sits 0.07–0.34% above the index. Indian traders on
Delta lean long and pay for it.

**The trade:** short the coin on the dear venue and long it on the cheap one,
in the same amount. Price moves cancel, and the funding gap is collected.

**Why it can exist.** Delta India is INR-only. Foreign arbitrageurs can't use
it, and an Indian who can must keep capital on two venues and face India's
crypto taxes. That is a barrier, and barriers keep gaps open.

**The test (rules fixed in advance)**, 2024-10 → 2026-09, all 190 coins
listed on both venues:

| | net per year | t | months positive | worst month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **as pre-registered** | **+97%** | 12.5 | **24 / 24** | +0.3% |
| the midnight payment credited conservatively | +97% | 13.1 | 24/24 | +1.0% |
| Delta's mark price instead of its last trade | +99% | 12.6 | 24/24 | +1.1% |
| costs ×5 | +86% | 10.0 | 21/24 | −2.1% |
| costs ×10 | +71% | 7.3 | 21/24 | −5.1% |
| only coins with ≥ $100k a day on Delta | +82% | 11.3 | 24/24 | +0.2% |
| only coins that are clearly the same asset on both | +94% | 13.3 | 24/24 | +0.3% |
| **all of the above at once (costs ×5)** | **+69%** | 9.3 | 21/24 | −2.2% |
| all of the above, costs ×10 | +57% | 6.7 | 19/24 | −5.3% |

Every half-year was positive: +18%, +37%, +77%, and +60% in the last six
months. A coin's 7-day gap predicts its next 7 days' gap (correlation +0.71).
I checked that Delta's funding records change only at exchange times, so the
test sums real settlement records, not predictions.

**It passed its bar, so it is now a paper ledger** (`C524CrossVenue`, the
"Delta vs Binance funding" panel):
- $500, once a day at 00:30 UTC;
- whole Delta contracts, the Binance leg matched;
- the real fees on both venues (Delta's with 18% GST);
- each leg's settled funding.

Run once on live data today, it found 89 coins with a gap of 20%/yr or more
and chose 10 (ORDER, AIN, KAITO, EDEN, AIO, MON, FARTCOIN, CROSS, IO, STBL),
all "short Delta / long Binance".

**Why this is NOT yet a money decision:**
1. **Tax, the decisive question.** If Binance's leg is taxed as a VDA (30%
   on gains, losses not offsettable) and Delta's as business income, the two
   price legs may be taxed unevenly. The prices cancel economically, but a
   gain on one leg could be taxed while the loss on the other cannot offset
   it. That could erase the edge. **Add this to the CA's questions (#8).**
2. **Two venues.** Capital must sit on both (INR on Delta, USDT on Binance),
   and moving money between them is slow and costs the 1% TDS. If one coin
   jumps, the losing leg's venue needs margin while the gain sits on the
   other: keep a buffer and low leverage.
3. **Small markets.** The coins it picks trade a median of about $50,000 a day
   on Delta, with $60–110k of open positions. Fine at $500 (a $50 leg), and
   workable to a few thousand dollars, not more. Spreads are 2–33 basis
   points, which the ×10-cost test covers.
4. **History is not the future.** Survivors only (coins delisted in 2024–26
   are missing). If more Indians arbitrage it, the gap shrinks. Delta can
   change its funding rules.

**What happens next:** the paper ledger runs every day on real data. After a
month we'll see whether real Delta prices and funding behave as the test
says. Live money only after (a) a clean paper record, (b) the CA's answer,
and (c) a small live trial on both venues.

**The honest bottom line:**
- **No passive product pays 2–4% a month.** The safe ones pay 0.4–0.65%.
  Anything claiming more passively is either a cycle that has passed
  (2024's airdrops) or hidden risk.
- **The book** remains the core: about 1.5–2% a month expected at the
  forecast Sharpe, with real bad months.
- **BFUSD** adds about 0.6% on the same capital.
- **The cross-venue funding spread is the first thing this project has found
  that, on paper, reaches 2–4% a month on its own without market
  direction.** It is also the one with the most practical catches. The paper
