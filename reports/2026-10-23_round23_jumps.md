## 2. The risk, with an analogy

Each pair is two opposite bets on one coin: "price falls" on one exchange and "price rises" on the other.
- When the coin jumps, the "falls" bet loses and the "rises" bet gains about the same, so **the pair as a whole is
  fine**.
- **The catch:** the two bets sit in **two different accounts**, and money can't jump between exchanges on its own.

**Analogy:** you own two shops in two towns. A flood in one town costs that shop money. The same weather makes the
other shop sell umbrellas at a profit.
- Across both shops, you're fine.
- But the flooded shop has to pay its bills **today**, from its own till. If its till runs dry before you can drive the
  money over, the bank closes that shop. The other shop's umbrella money doesn't help in time.

**What "the bank closes it" means here:**
- When an exchange account runs out of money, the exchange shuts every bet in it ("liquidation") and charges a fee.
- The matching bets on the other exchange are then left **unpaired**.
- If they are closed at once, the damage is mostly fees.
- If they are left open and the coin falls back, the loss can be most of the emptied account: up to about **half your
  money**.

**Why the automatic exits cost money.**

  - sells the losing bet a bit worse than its trigger (5% slip assumed);

- Without the exit, the pair just rides the jump out, because the two bets cancel.
- **Analogy:** an insurance policy that pays out on every strong gust of wind. Each claim costs you an excess, and the
  gusts come about 20 times a year, so the excesses add up to more than the storm it protects you from.

**What this means in money, at $1,000 ($500 a side, bets of about $100):**
- A **+100% jump** on the biggest bet takes that account to about 80%. That is ordinary, and the monthly even-out
  handles it.
- A **+500% jump** empties the account. The exchange closes all its bets. The other exchange's matching bets have
  gained about the same.
  - **If they are closed at once:** the cost is mostly fees: the exchange's liquidation fee plus closing fees. B2
    reads both exchanges' liquidation fees.
  - **If they are not closed and the coin falls back:** up to about **$400-500** is lost.
- So the protection that matters is not a filter on coins. It is **how the real-money code reacts in the minutes after
  a jump**. That is build step B2 (15 Nov).
