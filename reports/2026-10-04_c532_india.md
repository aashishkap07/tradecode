## 1. My tax position

| | Delta India / Pi42 (rupee-settled perps) | Binance (USDT-settled perps) |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | **speculative business income** (s.43(5)): futures settled without delivery, not on a recognised stock exchange | a **VDA** under s.115BBH: you receive USDT (a crypto) at settlement. That's the conservative, defensible reading |
| **Rate** | your slab + 4% cess, on the **year's net profit** | 30% + 4% cess (31.2%) on **each gain** |
| **Losses** | set off against other speculative gains; **carried forward 4 years** | **ignored**: no set-off, no carry-forward |
| **₹12 lakh rebate (s.87A, new regime)** | **yes**: it is normal-rate income | **no** (special-rate income) |
| **1% TDS (s.194S)** | none: no crypto changes hands | you would self-deposit it on a foreign exchange |
| **Sending money** | rupees, by bank | **outside the LRS**: Indian residents may not send money abroad for derivatives or margin trading (FEMA) |
| **Return** | ITR-3; keep each exchange's yearly P&L statement; pay advance tax if the year's tax tops ₹10,000 | — |

**What this means:**
- **The cross-venue trade's Binance half fails twice.** It's taxed as crypto
  (in my earlier study a 51–53%/yr drag on its capital), and the money can't
  legally go abroad for it.
- **So Binance stays paper only.** It is still the bot's data source,
  because Pi42 prices from it (§2).
- **Rupee futures on Delta and Pi42 are business income.** If your total
  income for the year is under ₹12 lakh, the rebate makes it **tax-free**.
- **Your setting:** the dashboard taxes at **31.2%** (the cautious default)
  until you set yours:
  - `C528_TAX_RATE = 0.0` if your total income is under ₹12 lakh;
  - `0.156` for ₹12–16 lakh;
  - `0.208` for 16–20 lakh;
  - `0.26` for 20–24 lakh;
  - `0.312` above 24 lakh.

## 2. Why Pi42 can replace Binance

- **What Pi42 is:** an Indian exchange, rupee-margined and rupee-settled.
  Its prices come from Binance (each contract quotes `"ps": "BTCUSDT"`).
- **I compared all 246 of its rupee perps against Binance, live, on 4 Oct:**
  - 216 settle funding at the **same moment**;
  - **87%** of their next rates are within 0.002% per settlement of
    Binance's;
  - the median gap is 0.0005%, against a typical rate of 0.005%.
  - Delta's rates are often far apart: DOGE 0.0533% vs 0.0019%, KAITO
    0.0295% vs 0.005%.
  - **So the gap the trade collects is still there between Delta and
    Pi42.**
- **What changes:**
  - **Fewer coins:** Pi42 lists 113 of Delta's (Binance: 190).
  - **Higher fees:** 0.10% + 18% GST against Binance's 0.05%.
  - **GST on funding:** I assume 18% on funding paid, to be cautious (Pi42's
    own API has a "GST on funding fee" entry).
- **The result:** on the stress-tested history (costs ×5, timing, Delta's
  mark, liquid coins only), **Delta vs Pi42 makes +40% to +49% a year
  (t 5.6–7.3)**, against +70% with Binance.
- **What I can't test:** Pi42's own funding history isn't public (only its
  live stream), so Binance's history stands in for it. The comparison above
  is the evidence. `research/c532_pi42_check.py` re-runs it any time;
  weekly is enough.

## 3. The split, with at least $50 kept back

**The method:** 20,000 random years. Returns are on the whole $600, after
tax as one business. The normal case is the stress-tested history; the
halved case halves the trade's edge again.

| reserve / book / Delta-vs-Pi42 | income < ₹12 L (0%) | 15.6% slab | 31.2% slab | edge halved (0% / 31.2%) | bad year (1 in 10) | losing year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **$50 / $0 / $550** | **3.79%, P 83%** | 3.29%, 78% | 2.76%, 70% | 1.93% / 1.37% | **+1.46%** | 2% |
| $50 / $100 / $450 | 3.17%, 78% | 2.75%, 71% | 2.30%, 60% | 1.68% / 1.19% | +1.27% | 1% |
| $50 / $200 / $350 | 2.76%, 74% | 2.38%, 64% | 1.98%, 49% | 1.81% / 1.28% | +1.23% | 1% |
| $50 / $550 / $0 | 2.26%, 55% | 1.94%, 49% | 1.61%, 41% | 2.26% / 1.61% | −0.47% | 14% |
| *(no reserve) $0 / $0 / $600* | 4.19%, 86% | 3.65%, 81% | 3.08%, 75% | 2.14% / 1.52% | +1.59% | 2% |

*The percentages are the typical year's monthly average after tax; P is
the chance a year averages at least 2% a month.*

**Why all $550 goes into the trade:**
- **It earns the most** in the normal case, and on the average of the
  normal and halved cases (2.86% at 0% tax).
- **The book has a 6-year record, but on Delta it holds only part of its
  plan** (whole contracts). Its typical year is lower and its losing years
  are 14%.
- **Adding it lowers your average month in the normal case.** If the
  trade's edge halved, the book alone would be slightly better (2.26% vs
  1.93%).
- **It keeps running on paper,** so we keep comparing.

**The cost of your $50 reserve:** about 0.4% a month (it earns nothing).
What it buys: a side that crashes gets topped up at once, before any
transfer arrives (§4).

**If "$50" meant $50 a month of profit:** that is 8.3% a month. In the
normal case a year averages that **only about 5% of the time**. Don't plan
on it.

  whenever one is below 65% of the two accounts' average, and once a month.
  It says exactly how much to move:

  > `C531 cross-venue (paper): the Delta side at 62% … -> Pi42 sends $54.50
  > to Delta (fee $1.00) … LIVE: make this transfer (rupees, by bank
  > transfer)`

- **On the history:**
  - the lowest an account fell was **48%** of its share;
  - one was below 50% in **4%** of months;
  - about **10 transfers a year**.
- **Below 50%, between runs:** the hourly check says **"top it up NOW"**.
  Your $25 of reserve on that exchange is the immediate cushion.
- **Moving money:** both exchanges take rupees by bank, so a transfer is a
  normal IMPS withdrawal and deposit, minutes to hours.
