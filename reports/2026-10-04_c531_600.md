# Your $600, no reserve: how to split it for the best average month (C531)

4 Oct 2026. You told me that:
- your **whole equity is $600**, with no reserve;
- you want it split for the **highest average monthly gain**.

You also asked whether the $100 should sit in Savings instead of Pendle.
**Yes:** at $100, Savings pays more than Pendle (C530 showed that). But with
$600 and no reserve there is no separate $100 any more, so **Pendle is
switched off** and every dollar goes into the plan.

**Short answer:**
- **$200 in the book on Delta India + $400 in the cross-venue trade** ($200
  on Delta, $200 on Binance).
- **Where the money sits:** $400 in your Delta India account and $200 in
  your Binance futures account.
- **What to expect,** if the tax works as the plan assumes:
  - about **4.5% a month** after tax at the 10.4% slab (~$27 a month),
    3.6% at the top slab;
  - a **94% chance** a year averages at least 2% a month (89% at the top
    slab).
- **Why not more in the cross-venue trade:** putting all $600 there earns
  more in the good case (5.5%). But it **loses about 1.2% a month** in the
  bad case: if India taxes its Binance leg as a VDA and the edge halves.
  The $200/$400 split roughly breaks even in that same case.

2. **No Savings on its idle cash.**

   - I had added Binance Savings to it, about 0.3% a month too much.

**After both corrections, the book on Delta at $500 makes:**

- **+2.69%** in the planning case (the backtest cut by a third), not

## 2. Every split of $600

**How this is measured:**
- **The data:** 2020–26 history for the book; 2024–26, stress-tested, for
  the cross-venue trade.
- **Both in whole Delta contracts** at their own size.
- **The method:** 20,000 random years.
- **Tax:** on profits only.
- **The script:** `research/c531_split_600.py/.txt`.

**Four cases, because two things are still unknown:**
- **A: tax as the plan assumes.** Both parts are business income: a year's
  net profit is taxed at your slab, and a loss is not taxed.
- **B: India treats the cross-venue trade's Binance leg as crypto (a
  VDA).** Every winning leg is taxed 31.2% and losses don't count. Its
  Delta leg stays business income. This is the open question for your CA.
- **Planning:** the cross-venue edge as stress-tested.
- **Pessimistic:** that edge halved again.

**The median year's monthly average after tax, at the 10.4% slab** (in
brackets: the chance a year averages ≥ 2% a month):

- $0/$600: 4.49% (92%);

**How to read this, as a trader would:**
- **The cross-venue trade is the engine.** In case A every dollar moved
  into it raises the average. All $600 there is the maximum: 5.5% a month.
- **Its weak point is tax, not the market.** If the Binance leg is a VDA,
  the tax on its winning legs eats most of its edge, because the legs' price
  swings are large even when the pair is flat. All-in, that case earns 1.75%
  a month; with the edge also halved, −1.19%.
- **The book alone is the steadiest across the four cases** (2.31% in
  every one). But it **can't reach your target**: only a 56% chance of a 2%
  average, and a losing year 1 time in 7.
- **$200/$400 balances the two:**
  - it is within 0.03% a month of the best four-case average;
  - it meets your >75% target in case A (94% / 89%);
  - its worst case still roughly breaks even (+0.12%);
  - all-in cross-venue loses 1.2% a month in that same worst case.
- **The size of the parts matters too:**
  - **$400 in the cross-venue trade:** legs of $40 clear more coins' one
    contract than legs of $30. It makes +70.5% a year stress-tested,
    against +58.9% at $300.
  - **$200 in the book:** still holds about 6 coins.
  - **$100/$500 is worse than $200/$400 in three of the four cases.** It
    is better only in case A (4.76% vs 4.51%), because at $100 the book
    holds only 37% of its plan.

## 3. No reserve: how the cross-venue trade protects itself

**The problem:**
- The trade is two accounts: shorts on one venue, longs on the other.
- When a coin rallies hard (AIN +122% on 4 Oct), the short side loses
  while the long side gains the same.
- **Without a reserve, the only rescue is moving money from the winning
  side to the losing side.**

**On the history ($400, whole contracts, daily closes):**

| rule | lowest a side fell | months a side was < 50% of its half | transfers a year |
|---|---|---|---|
| even out once a month only | **24.5%** (close to liquidation) | **21%** | 0 |
| **monthly + whenever a side is below 65% of the two sides' mean** | **57.3%** | **4%** | ~8 |

**The rule now in the bot (C531):**
- **Who sends:** at the daily run (06:00 IST), if a side is below 65% of
  the two sides' mean, the richer side sends half the difference. It also
  happens on the first run of each month if they differ by more than 2%.
- **The cost:** $1 a transfer, paid by the sender. About 8 a year is $8,
  or 0.11% a month on $600.
- **On paper** the ledger does it itself.
- **Live, you make the transfer.** The log says exactly how much and which
  way, for example:

  > `C531 cross-venue (paper): the Delta side at 62% of the two sides'
  > mean -> Binance sends $54.50 to Delta (fee $1.00) ... LIVE: make this
  > transfer`

- **The hourly watch:** it now names the amount too. Below 50% it says
  **"move $X from Binance to Delta NOW (do not wait for the daily run)"**.
  Daily closes understate fast moves: AIN rose 20% within hours.

**Before going live, check one thing:**
- **The question:** how fast USDT moves between Binance and your Delta
  India account, and at what fee.
- **Why:** the rule assumes within a day for about $1.
- **If it is slower:** the rule needs to start earlier (a higher threshold
  than 65%). Tell me the time and fee and I'll re-run the history.

## 5. What changes on the server

When you update:
- **The Delta book is rebased $500 → $200 (×0.4).**
  - Every figure and position is scaled exactly, so its value is ×0.4 at
    any price.
  - At Delta's next prices (within 5 minutes) each position is rounded to
    whole contracts. Anything under half a contract closes; on the 3 Oct
    ledger that was BTC and BNB. Its value doesn't move.
  - Its month guard keeps its percentage (anchor ×0.4).
- **The cross-venue ledger is rebased to $400.**
  - ×0.8 from $500 (or ×1.6 from $250, if C530 was deployed first).
  - AIN goes 21 → 17 contracts.
- **The plan is $600:**
  - the top tile reads "Your plan · paper … of $600";
  - the risk tile says "no reserve: evened out from the other venue below
    65%";
  - the budget line says "your equity $600".
- **Pendle is off.** Its panel says why.
